What the bank told Mumbai police (complaint, 22 Oct; FIR for cheating by personation; reported 24–25 Oct): unknown persons used the national SFMS messaging system to push 187 unauthorised RTGS credits. Sender records in those messages do not belong to the bank, per its complaint.

**3/**
What IFTAS, the RBI subsidiary that runs SFMS, said (22 Oct, reported 25 Oct): no unauthorised access to the SFMS hub, and no security breach. Both statements cannot be equally complete. That is the finding that stands.

**4/**
SFMS is the hub through which RTGS and NEFT messages travel between 300+ banks. Bank side, each member runs a gateway on its own servers. Access rides on INFINET, the closed financial network, and IDRBT-CA digital certificates.

**5/**
Passwords still open the doors on the bank side. We read the RTGS System Regulations (Oct 2024), the IDRBT-CA certificate practice statement, and RBI's access circulars. We found no published circular that mandates multi-factor authentication for SFMS console logins.

**6/**
What we did not find: any public statement from RBI or CERT-In. No recovery figure. No account-freeze count. The 187 transfers are per the bank's complaint as reported. IFTAS disputes the breach vector. We grade these separately for a reason.

**7/**

1. CBI: release SFMS access logs, Oct 15–17.
2. IFTAS: did the hub see the 187 messages, and from which gateway?
3. RBI: is MFA on SFMS console access mandatory now?

The logs name the door. Publishing settles it.
