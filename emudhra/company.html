The company behind the keys: a consumer history of eMudhra

Draft for review, 21 October 2026. Numbers and quotes are sourced in the footnotes; what we verified ourselves is marked as our own verification.

We are CashlessConsumer, a consumer collective. This page documents the company at the centre of our statement and open letter: who founded it, who runs it, what it sells, who buys, and where the money comes from. Every figure is sourced. Nothing here alleges wrongdoing; this is corporate record, assembled so consumers can judge the institution that holds their trust.

The people

V. Srinivasan founded eMudhra in 2008 and remains Executive Chairman.8 He holds a mathematics degree from the University of Madras and is a member of all three Indian accounting and secretarial bodies: the Institutes of Chartered Accountants, Cost Accountants, and Company Secretaries.9 Co-founder Kaushik Srinivasan is a Whole-time Director.10

The promoters are V. Srinivasan and Taarav Pte. Limited, a Singapore holding entity.11 At listing, the offer for sale also included shares from Kaushik Srinivasan, Lakshmi Kaushik, Arvind Srinivasan, and Aishwarya Arvind, a family group selling alongside the promoters.12

The executive bench: Venu Madhava (Executive VP, Legal, GRC and HR; Whole-time Director), Ritesh Raj Pariyani (CFO), and Johnson Xavier (Company Secretary).13 The CCA’s disclosure record lists the wider board, including Dr. Nandlal Laxminarayan Sarda, Manoj Kunkalienkar, Chandra Lakshminarayan Iyer, and Chandrasekar Padmanabhan.14

The money

FY2026 revenue was ₹7,016 million across three lines.24 Enterprise Solutions, 59% of revenue, grew 55% year on year: certificate lifecycle management (CertInext), identity and access management (SecurePass), and the emSigner paperless workflow, which the company says processes over 350,000 transactions a day in banking and capital markets.2526 Services contributed 21%. Trust Services, the licensed certifying-authority business, contributed 20%, of which 94% is the retail digital signature certificate and eSign business.27

The geography has shifted fast: India 36%, North America 34%, Europe 12%, Middle East and Africa 11%, Asia Pacific 7%.28 By customer sector, Government and Defence take 32% of revenue, Enterprise 41%, and BFSI 27%.29

Pre-IPO, the company raised US$26.2 million from Motilal Oswal, Hornbill Capital, and 34 other investors.30

Who profits from being trusted

Three numbers carry the consumer story.

₹251.79 crore of the ₹412.79 crore IPO was promoters selling. The fresh money that reached the company was ₹161 crore; the larger half went to exiting shareholders, led by Taarav Pte. Limited (45.16 lakh shares) and V. Srinivasan (32.89 lakh shares).31

The retail certificate is a mandated purchase. A two-year digital signature certificate costs about ₹1,500, and certifying authorities keep 45–50% as commission under the CA-led distribution model, which is about 70% of volume.32 Over 50 million certificates have been issued since inception, and income-tax filing, MCA filings, and tenders are the volume drivers.3334 Consumers buy these because the law routes them through a CA; they do not shop for them.

A third of revenue comes from government and defence. The same state whose regulators stayed silent after the October 2026 key failure is the company’s largest sector buyer, at 32% of FY26 revenue.35

The trust stack, and what broke

eMudhra operates two trust hierarchies. The India hierarchy runs on the CCA licence of 7 November 2008 and issues the retail DSC and eSign certificates.36 The international hierarchy runs under emSign, on WebTrust-accredited roots, now expanded into Europe through CRYPTAS’s eIDAS qualification.37 The eight compromised keys sit on the international side. Our own desk verification adds one more dependency: IDRBT, the RBI-established institute that runs the banking sector’s certifying authority, builds its issuance on eMudhra’s emCA platform, so the banking PKI borrows the vendor’s stack even where it holds its own licence.38

That is the structural point for consumers. This is not one compromised product line. It is one vendor whose platform issues the state’s identity certificates, the banks’ PKI, and a growing share of European trust services, and whose failure surfaced through foreign browsers and foreign root programs rather than through any Indian authority. The corporate record above is the context for the four asks in our open letter.
