🛡️ DPI chain verification · welfare-fraud pattern

5.02 crore mobile numbers, one worker's password

Pregnant women in Ahmedabad are answering calls from people who know their name, pregnancy stage, and exact ₹12,000 maternity entitlement — then losing ₹4,000–₹90,000 to OTP fraud. The callers cite the women's Poshan Tracker records. Poshan Tracker is the government's nutrition database: 8.96 crore beneficiaries, of whom 5.02 crore have been seeded with ABHA health IDs — a card that carries the beneficiary's mobile number — issued through 13 lakh anganwadi workers' handheld phones. We verified the plumbing from the app binary up. This is not a hack. It is a consent-desert design whose trust anchor is the weakest device at the edge.

What happened in Ahmedabad

Divya Bhaskar and Bhaskar English reported on 16 November 2026 that Ahmedabad cyber-police had received 10+ complaints from pregnant women who got calls from people posing as ASHA workers, anganwadi didis or ICDS officials. The callers offered the women's real entitlements — a "Poshan Tracker lottery", or the ₹12,000 maternity assistance — and collected OTPs. Losses ran ₹4,000 to ₹90,000. One woman in her eighth month lost ₹42,000 saved for her delivery. Police arrested two people carrying multiple SIM cards.

An ICDS supervisor has moved the Gujarat High Court, arguing that anganwadi workers' Poshan Tracker login credentials are being compromised — that the stolen access is used to download beneficiaries' ABHA cards, which show the beneficiary's full mobile number — and that the numbers fuel the impersonation calls. She also said ASHA workers' CUG (closed-user-group) numbers were easily available on the Health Department's website, giving fraudsters the perfect pretext to pose as ASHA.

The same reporter's sweep found the pattern outside Gujarat: Rajkot (women called about PMMVY and "Ladki Bahin" money, told to install a bank app from a WhatsApp link and screenshot their balance; one lost ₹90,000; NIC probe sought), Bhopal (Aug 2025) (Poshan Tracker data suspected sold at ~₹400 a record; fake PMMVY ₹5,000 calls; workers ordered to change passwords), Sant Kabir Nagar, UP (₹5,000-scheme calls quoting beneficiary details), and Galudih, Jharkhand (women posing as anganwadi workers emptied ₹18,000 via UPI).

Claim levels: these are press-reported cases (L2–L3) from one publisher family for Gujarat, plus independently reported precedents. The charge sheets that would prove the data trail are not public. We separate what is reported from what we verified — below.

First, the three systems — a 90-second explainer

  • Poshan Tracker — the Ministry of Women & Child Development's mobile app (rolled out March 2021 through NeGD). Every anganwadi worker runs it on her phone to manage her centre: beneficiaries (pregnant women, lactating mothers, children 0–6), growth tracking, take-home ration distribution (with facial-recognition verification), and vaccination. It is now India's largest nutrition database.
  • ABHA — the Ayushman Bharat Health Account, a 14-digit health ID from the National Health Authority (ABDM). The downloaded ABHA card carries name, photo, date of birth, gender, ABHA number, a QR code — and the mobile number.
  • The money — PMMVY pays ₹5,000 for a first living child (₹6,000 for a second girl child); Gujarat adds its own ₹12,000 maternity support (Namo Shree Yojana). These amounts go to the woman's bank account after verification — which is exactly why "your ₹12,000 has come, share the OTP to receive it" is such an effective lie.

The chain we verified — from the government's own paper and the app binary

We did two things: pulled the government's own statements and official app documentation, and statically analysed the Poshan Tracker Android app (v25.3.16, from a public mirror). No government system was probed or logged into. Nothing here required one.

The design assumption — and its failure mode

The architecture routes five crore women's identity documents through thirteen lakh shared, low-security-margin Android phones, held by workers who are themselves poorly paid, thinly trained in security, and whose login sessions were — until recently — allegedly sold by the hundred. The system's identity anchor is the weakest device at the edge; its caller-pretext is the government's own benefit amount; and the one field that turns a stolen record into a phone call — the mobile number — is printed on the very card the workflow downloads.

Every documented incident is edge-side: the worker's credentials, the worker's phone, the beneficiary's number. Nothing reported suggests Poshan Tracker's servers or ABDM's hub were breached — and that is precisely the point. A system can be uncompromised at the centre and still leak, because the leak channel is its own last-mile workforce.

The response so far, where there has been one, has been to tell anganwadi workers to change their passwords. The burden of a five-crore-record architecture has landed on the worker — not on the design.

What we could not verify

  • The leak mechanism. Credential theft, insider sale, session hijack on the worker's phone — no forensics are public. The ₹400-per-record figure from Bhopal is a police suspicion, not an established market price.
  • The exact card rendering in Gujarat's files. Whether the ABHA cards downloaded through Poshan Tracker in Ahmedabad show the full number, or the partially masked rendering, is untested by us. It needs one specimen card — a Narasimha-scope question, not a passive one.
  • Consent posture. Whether beneficiaries meaningfully consented to ABHA seeding — or to their number appearing on a downloadable card — is not public anywhere. The Rajya Sabha answer describes integration, not consent.
  • Breach-reporting trail. No evidence either way on whether any of these incidents reached CERT-In or, since 13 Nov 2025, the Data Protection Board.
  • Gujarat's ASHA CUG directory. District health societies across India commonly publish ASHA mobile-number lists; we did not locate the live Gujarat page in passive checks.

If you are a beneficiary — or her family

  • No government scheme sends money by OTP. PMMVY, Namo Shree, and every maternity scheme deposit to the bank account you gave at registration. Anyone who calls saying "share this OTP / install this app / screen-share to receive your ₹12,000" is stealing from you. Full stop.
  • Real officials already have your details — that is how the scam works. A caller knowing your name, pregnancy month, or scheme amount proves nothing. Anyone asking you to act (OTP, app, UPI "verification") is the fraudster.
  • Verify by walking, not by trusting the incoming call. Go to your anganwadi centre or PHC and ask. Do not call back the number that called you.
  • If money has left: call 1930 immediately (or report at cybercrime.gov.in) within the golden hour so banks can freeze the mule account. Keep the transaction SMS. Then file with the local cyber police.

If you are an anganwadi worker or supervisor

  • Your Poshan Tracker login is the system's front door. Use a unique password, not the one on your WhatsApp groups.
  • Do not photograph beneficiary profiles for "report" purposes; do not leave sessions open on shared devices; report lost devices the same day.
  • If you are asked (by anyone) to bulk-download ABHA cards or export lists outside the app workflow — that request itself is the red flag. Refuse and report it.

What would settle this

  • RTI to MoWCD / NeGD: Poshan Tracker security audits (CERT-In-empanelled auditor reports), session and device controls for AWW logins, MDM (mobile device management) status, and any fraud-linkage assessment done before scaling ABHA seeding.
  • RTI to NHA / ABDM: the ABHA card field policy — what renders on cards generated through partner applications (like Poshan Tracker), full vs masked mobile display, and the change history of that masking.
  • RTI to WCD Gujarat / Ahmedabad ICDS: the data-leak audit following the officer's High Court representation, ABHA-seeding SOP and consent artefacts, and counts of beneficiary-data incidents by district.
  • Police: the Ahmedabad cyber cell charge sheet — CDR analysis of the arrested callers' numbers against the victims' entries would name the actual source of the beneficiary lists (Poshan Tracker exports, ABHA card downloads, or a district directory). That single document settles the mechanism question.
  • One specimen card from the affected workflow would settle the field-display question in minutes.
